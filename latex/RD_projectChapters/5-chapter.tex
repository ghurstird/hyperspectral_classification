\subsection{Performance Change Analysis}
The accuracy change relative to the supervised baseline is calculated as follows:

\begin{equation}
    \text{Change\%} = \frac{\text{OA}_{\text{alt}} - \text{OA}_{\text{Supervised}}}{\text{OA}_{\text{Supervised}}} \times 100
    \label{eq:improvement}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Semi-Supervised vs. Supervised}
Semi-supervised learning shows substantial performance degradation compared to the supervised baseline across all datasets, most severely on the small Indian Pines dataset:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Indian Pines:} Average drop of $-34.96$ percentage points across architectures (from 99.81\% to 64.85\%), indicating that the self-training strategy is highly sensitive to limited dataset size and class imbalance.
    \item \textbf{Pavia University:} Average drop of $-4.63$ percentage points (from 99.91\% to 95.28\%), demonstrating that curriculum learning partially mitigates degradation when larger unlabeled pools are available.
    \item \textbf{Salinas:} Average drop of $-6.46$ percentage points (from 99.88\% to 93.42\%), suggesting that even the largest dataset is insufficient to recover fully supervised-level accuracy through pseudo-labeling alone.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Federated vs. Supervised}
Federated learning achieves near-supervised performance on larger datasets while showing a more notable gap on Indian Pines:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Indian Pines:} Average difference of $-8.26$ percentage points (from 99.81\% to 91.55\%), reflecting the challenge of federated aggregation on a small dataset with limited local samples per client.
    \item \textbf{Pavia University:} Average difference of $-0.40$ percentage points (from 99.91\% to 99.51\%), demonstrating strong robustness across heterogeneous urban land-cover classes.
    \item \textbf{Salinas:} Average difference of $-0.75$ percentage points (from 99.88\% to 99.13\%), showing near-parity performance at scale.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Key Findings:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{2D-CNN Highest Overall Accuracy:} The 2D-CNN architecture achieves the highest average supervised accuracy (99.96\%), the highest semi-supervised average (89.22\%), and the highest federated average (98.72\%) across all three datasets. Despite being the simplest architecture, 2D convolutions appear sufficient to capture discriminative spectral-spatial information after PCA-based dimensionality reduction.

    \item \textbf{3D-CNN Consistent Robustness:} The 3D-CNN achieves the second-highest performance across all paradigms (supervised: 99.91\%, semi-supervised: 86.92\%, federated: 97.36\%). Its volumetric convolution operations capture inter-band correlations that contribute to stable performance under both semi-supervised and federated settings.

    \item \textbf{ViT Greatest Sensitivity to Distribution Shift:} Vision Transformer models achieve competitive supervised accuracy (99.72\%) but exhibit the largest degradation under semi-supervised (77.41\% average) and federated (94.11\% average) paradigms, indicating that the self-attention mechanism is more sensitive to noisy pseudo-labels and weight aggregation across heterogeneous data distributions.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Dataset-Specific Analysis}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Indian Pines:} Smallest dataset (10,249 samples) benefits most from semi-supervised learning (+1.63\% average improvement), indicating effectiveness in limited-label scenarios.
    
    \item \textbf{Pavia University:} Urban classification task (42,776 samples) shows robust performance across paradigms, with federated learning achieving only -1.01\% degradation, suggesting suitability for multi-institutional collaboration scenarios.
    
    \item \textbf{Salinas:} Largest dataset (54,129 samples) demonstrates highest absolute accuracy (98.67\% semi-supervised 3D-CNN) and narrowest federated-supervised gap (-0.67\%), showing scalability to large hyperspectral datasets.
\end{itemize}
